01 About

AI transformation is an adoption problem before it is a technology problem.

I work with business leaders on AI transformation. The pattern repeats across finance, operations, and IT. The models are good enough. The hard part is choosing the right work, connecting the tool to the systems and data it needs, and getting the people who own the process to trust the output.

How I work

  • Start with the daily work, not the demo.
  • Pick the first project by frequency and pain, not novelty.
  • Decide who approves what before anything touches a system of record.
  • Measure adoption and cycle time, not activity.
  • Keep people on the decisions that matter.
The work that runs a company crosses apps. It carries state across weeks and quarters. It runs while you sleep. And when it writes to a system of record, someone approves it first.

03 Results

Projects and what they returned.

Workflow and transformation projects with clients, and the business impact. Names removed, details changed.

01

Qualify the contractor before underwriting does

Home improvement lender, about 10,000 contractors on its network

The growth team had built a lead agent themselves. It pulled contractors from a data vendor by trade, revenue band, and geography, checked them against the CRM, enrolled them in email sequences, and opened an opportunity for each one. It worked, and it had two problems. It was blind to any contractor not in the vendor's database, and more than half the applications it produced were declined in underwriting, fifteen days after the contractor applied.

We put a research layer ahead of the vendor pull. Perplexity searches the public web for contractors that fit the profile, then checks each one against the lender's acceptance criteria before anyone pays for a record. Licensing, years in business, review history, complaint records, bankruptcy filings, and a working phone and email. The CRM check moved from exact name matching to a scored match that a person reviews when it is close. Enrichment reads the contractor's own site, Facebook page, and Google reviews to draft the first email.

Nothing sends and nothing writes to the CRM until a rep approves the batch.

Sixty-day pilot, 5,000 leads. Approval rate on submitted applications went from 45 percent to 82 percent. Cost per qualified lead fell 38 percent because fewer records were bought. Cold email engagement held above 1 percent. One in five qualified contractors was not in the data vendor at all.

02

Month-end close, three days shorter

Multi-site healthcare services group, about 400 people

Finance closed the books in nine business days. Most of that was invoice matching and intercompany reconciliations done by hand from ERP exports.

We mapped the close step by step with the controller, then built a workflow that three-way matches invoices to purchase orders and receipts, routes exceptions to a review queue, and drafts the reconciliation memos. Nothing posts to the ERP until the controller approves it.

Close went from nine days to six. About 1,400 invoices a month match without a person touching them. Exceptions run near six percent. Two accountants moved from matching to analysis.

03

2,300 leases, abstracted and watched

Commercial real estate owner and operator

Leases lived as PDFs. Rent steps, option windows, and expirations were tracked in spreadsheets by whoever had time. Notices got missed.

We built a workflow that reads each lease into a structured abstract, writes it to the property management system once a paralegal approves it, and sets alerts on every critical date. The paralegal reviews the abstract against the source pages side by side.

All 2,300 leases abstracted in six weeks, against an internal estimate of fourteen months by hand. No missed renewal notices in the two quarters since. Two option windows caught early, worth about 1.8 million dollars in rent.

04

Investor reporting without the scramble

Private credit manager, 40 funds

Every quarter, four people spent two weeks producing investor letters and capital account statements. One restatement the prior year had cost a difficult call with a large LP.

We connected the fund administration data, built a templated drafting workflow, and put a reviewer gate on every number. Each figure carries a link back to the ledger entry it came from.

The quarterly cycle went from ten business days to three. The team took on two new funds without adding headcount. No restatements since.

05

A proposal desk for a consulting firm

Environmental and safety consultancy, about 250 people

Requests for proposals arrived by email and got answered on senior consultants' evenings. Win rate sat at 22 percent, and good bids were skipped for lack of time.

We built a capture workflow that scans the inbox for RFPs, scores each one against the firm's go and no-go rubric, assembles a first draft from an approved answer library, and routes it to a principal for edits. The principal decides what goes out.

First drafts in two hours instead of two days. Proposals submitted up 40 percent. Win rate at 31 percent within two quarters.

06

Regulatory change, read once, acted on

Regional bank, about 750 people

Compliance spent about twelve hours a week reading regulator sites and mapping changes to internal policies. Memos lagged the changes by weeks.

We built a monitoring workflow that scans the agreed sources, matches each change to the policies it touches, and drafts an impact memo with citations. A compliance officer reviews and signs each memo before it is filed.

Monitoring went from twelve hours a week to two. Every memo cites its source. The next internal audit found no unmapped changes.
